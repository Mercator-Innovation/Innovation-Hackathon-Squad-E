# Ctrl-Alt-Elite: showcase (Wed 25 Nov, 10-minute slot)

**Challenge 2: Help when it's needed.** Our prototype is **Night Neighbours**.
**Presenters:** Roqiya Hassan, Daniel Lambert-Maddocks, Bose Adeyemi and Kai Jones. 9 minutes presenting, then 1 minute for the questionnaire. If Bose or Kai can't make it, Roqiya or Daniel covers their section.

**Before the call:** tell Claude `get the latest and make it run for me`. Open these tabs: the persona board (`sketches/persona-board.html`), the no-phone video (`src/no-phone-video/`), **See a help point**, **Ask by text**, the **coordinator view**, **Spreading the word**.

---

## 0:00 to 1:00 · The problem (Roqiya)

- "Most support closes at five. The night is when people affected by substance use feel most alone."
- "We focused on isolated men aged 35 to 54 in deprived and rural areas, where help is hardest to reach."

## 1:00 to 2:15 · Our personas: not just one user (Roqiya, show the persona board)

- "We didn't design for one person. We designed for three: **Jamie**, a person who uses drugs; **Karen**, his sister; and a **support worker**."
- "Each has a 24-hour clock that shows when they need help and when help is actually there. The gap is at night, for all three."

## 2:15 to 3:30 · Not just digital: help when you're offline (Daniel, play the video, then click)

- Play the **no-phone video**. It's Jamie's night, in English, Welsh and Gaelic.
- **See a help point**: "Help points by the parcel lockers at late-night supermarkets. Charge your phone, pick up a donated phone, or press one big button."

## 3:30 to 4:30 · No app, no data, low battery (Bose, show Ask by text)

- "I asked Claude how to reach the hardest to reach. The answer was a text-only demo: any old phone, no data. Text one word, TALK, COME or BED."
- "I'm a business analyst, but I walked it through as Jamie, found gaps and fixed them in the code myself, along with the accessibility problems we found."

## 4:30 to 5:30 · Showing councils where help is needed (Kai, show the coordinator view)

- "The coordinator view shows councils anonymised totals: which areas are asking for help, and how many more volunteers each one needs."
- "No names, ever. And 'How these numbers are made' sits right next to the figures, so people can see why an area is flagged."

## 5:30 to 6:30 · Bringing in big companies, and spreading the word (Roqiya)

- "Supermarkets and big companies host the help points and donate phones. It helps people, and it's good for their name in the community."
- **Spreading the word**: "I built our campaign: posters for GP surgeries, beer mats, social posts, and wallet cards for pharmacy bags. None of it says 'drugs', so the card is safe to carry."

## 6:30 to 7:30 · How it went, and what didn't work (Daniel, then Roqiya)

- **Daniel:** "Five of us, none of us developers, built about 19 pages in under four hours. Our ideas merged seamlessly between each other's work."
- **Roqiya:** "The first thing 'Jamie' texted was HELP, and the reply was 'Sorry, we didn't get that.' We fixed it. But it's built around one man's night, and nothing has been tested with real people yet."

## 7:30 to 8:15 · Outside our usual jobs (one line each)

- **Daniel**, a service designer, made the voiced video in three languages. **Roqiya**, a user researcher, built a marketing campaign.
- **Bose**, a business analyst, fixed code and accessibility. **Kai**, our merge captain, kept everyone's work together on one shared screen. **Sruthi**, our other user researcher, turned research into personas and working screens.

## 8:15 to 9:00 · What Mercator should take from this (Bose)

- "Hackathons can be for multidisciplinary teams, not just coders. Keep them mixed, with different points of view."
- "Next time: maybe less time to build, and more time for show and tell. Thank you. We'll leave a minute for the questionnaire."
