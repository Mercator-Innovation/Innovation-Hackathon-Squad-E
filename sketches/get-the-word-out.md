# Get the word out: reaching people who aren't online

*Sruthi James, Ctrl-Alt-Elite, 22 October. Built into the prototype as `src/outreach.html` (a wallet card, a poster with tear-off strips, and where they go).*

## Where it goes

- **People who might need help:** wallet cards in naloxone kits and pharmacy bags. Posters at needle exchanges, food banks, hostels and the job centre. Cards handed out when leaving hospital or prison.
- **Through people they trust:** peer workers and volunteers hand out cards and tell people face to face. The research found low trust in services, so this matters most.
- **At the help points:** the locker screen shows Night Neighbours when no one is using it, with a poster in the shop next door. Daniel's supermarket billboards fit here.
- **Families and friends:** GP surgeries, schools, churches, community councils, local radio and local Facebook groups.
- **Everyone else:** bus stops, beer mats, the back of shop receipts. The number, everywhere.

## Our pick

Wallet cards in naloxone kits and pharmacy bags, plus peers telling people. Both reach someone like Jamie face to face, with no phone or internet.

## Design choices

- The card and poster never say "drugs", so the card is safe to keep in a wallet.
- The back of the card has space for a check-in number and who to tell, so the plan works without a phone.
- Tear-off strips on the poster mean people can take the number away without being seen writing it down.
