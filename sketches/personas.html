Squad E · Challenge 2 · Help when it's needed

Three people, one long night

A person who uses drugs, someone in their family, and a support worker. Each poster shows when help is open, when they need it most, and what gets in the way.

⚠These are made-up people. They come from desk research done on 22 October 2026, not from talking to anyone. Treat every detail as an assumption to test.
How they connect: Karen is Jamie's sister. Priya was Jamie's key worker until he stopped coming in. All three are part of Jamie's "circle of support".

What gets in the way

  • Shame about using again [1]
  • Doesn't trust services after a bad experience [2]
  • No data, low battery, weak signal [4]
  • Nearest service is two buses away [7]
  • Worried about who will find out [3]

Dots show how much each thing gets in the way. They're our guess, not measured.

What Jamie needs

  • Someone to talk to at night who won't judge him
  • A way to reach out that works with no data and little battery
  • To feel less alone, without it turning into an emergency
  • To choose who knows, including his sister

A night in Karen's life

  1. 20:00Finishes her shift. Texts Jamie. No reply.
  2. 22:30Calls twice. Straight to voicemail: his phone's probably dead.
  3. 23:45Searches online for help. Finds services that open at 9am.
  4. 00:30Hasn't told friends. Feels judged too. Stays up worrying.

What gets in the way

  • Doesn't know what help exists at night [7]
  • Unsure if it's serious enough for 999 [7]
  • Shame: the family keeps it quiet [1]
  • Can't reach Jamie when his phone dies [4]
  • Worn out, with no support for herself [7]

Dots show how much each thing gets in the way. They're our guess, not measured.

What Karen needs

  • To know what to do tonight, step by step
  • Someone to talk to about how she's feeling
  • To be part of Jamie's support, if he agrees
  • To learn how to keep him safer, such as naloxone (a medicine that reverses an opioid overdose)

A day in Priya's life

  1. 10:00Drives to a visit. Jamie isn't in. Leaves a card.
  2. 14:30Rings three services to find out who opens at weekends.
  3. 16:55A client calls, upset. She has five minutes before her shift ends.
  4. Mon 09:00Finds out a client went to A&E on Saturday night.

What gets in the way

  • No one to hand over to after 17:00 [7]
  • Support split across many organisations [2]
  • Clients' phones die or change number [4]
  • Rules on sharing sensitive information [3]
  • Hears about weekend crises too late [7]

Dots show how much each thing gets in the way. They're our guess, not measured.

What Priya needs

  • To hand someone over safely at the end of the day
  • To know who is covering tonight, and where
  • To hear the next morning what happened, with the person's consent
  • To show where the gaps in cover are

Priya stands in for all support workers: peer workers, charity outreach staff, NHS and Alcohol and Drug Partnership teams.

Where this comes from

Small numbers in square brackets point to these sources. Names, ages, places, phones and timelines are invented to make the research feel real.

  1. UK Drug Policy Commission: Getting serious about stigma in Scotland (2010)
  2. University of Edinburgh: Preventing drug-related deaths in Scotland, primary care study
  3. Survey of Scottish treatment providers on barriers (preprint, not yet peer-reviewed)
  4. Digital Health: Digital Lifelines supports vulnerable people in Scotland (2024), and the Harm Reduction Journal evaluation (2024, small sample)
  5. Public Health Scotland: drug-related deaths database, 2019 and 2020. 76% of people were living in their own home and 63% lived alone. That's why Jamie lives alone.
  6. Pharmaceutical Journal on the 2024 figures. Opioids were involved in 80% of drug misuse deaths and benzodiazepines in 56%. That's why Jamie uses both.
  7. Our challenge sheet, based on CivTech Challenge 12.1
