# Ctrl-Alt-Elite: the idea and its assumptions

Challenge 2: Help when it's needed. Agreed at the talk-through on 22 October, blending the hub idea with Sruthi and Daniel's help points (`combined-idea-help-points.md`). Live copy on the [Discovery Wall](https://claude.ai/artifact/5FGTjyre9Q3kxepJYxt5Kw).

## The idea

For people affected by substance use, especially isolated men aged 35 to 54 in deprived and rural areas, when they feel alone in the evening and at night: a community support service with an app and a text fallback. They can find and join a local hub to meet people and be heard, or send an SOS (by app, or a short text if they’ve no data or low battery) to vetted community volunteers nearby, who respond, meet them or bring them to the hub, and pass them to professional help if it escalates. Family and support workers can stay connected, and anonymised totals show councils where more help is needed.

## Assumptions to test

1. Men aged 35 to 54 in deprived areas are the group most at risk from substance use in Scotland.
2. They feel loneliest in the evenings and at night, when most support is closed.
3. Isolation is a big part of what leads to harm, so meeting people and being heard would help.
4. They'd go to a physical local hub, and would trust it if it doesn't feel like a statutory or crisis service.
5. There are local spaces that could open in the evenings, and people or services to staff them.
6. They have a phone with enough data, battery and signal, and would use an app to find the hub.
7. They'd want their family and support workers involved, and would agree to share what's needed with them.
8. Enough people would volunteer, and can be vetted, trained and kept safe when they respond at night.
9. People would trust a volunteer from their own community more than a statutory service.

Only number 1 has been checked so far (below). The rest still need evidence or testing with real people.

## Evidence for assumption 1: mostly holds

From the National Records of Scotland, drug misuse deaths in 2024 ([NRS](https://nrscotland.gov.uk/publications/drug-related-deaths-in-scotland-2024)):

- 63% were people aged 35 to 54.
- Men were more than twice as likely to die as women.
- People in the most deprived areas were 12 times as likely to die as people in the least deprived.

We didn't find the figure for men aged 35 to 54 in deprived areas on its own. It should be in the NRS detailed tables.

## Who else is working on this (checked 22 October 2026)

- **Digital Lifelines Scotland** (DHI, SCVO and Simon Community Scotland): phones, data and digital skills for people who use drugs. More than 1,700 people supported, including projects in North and South Ayrshire. ([FutureScot](https://futurescot.com/pioneering-digital-lifelines-scotland-programme-secures-new-funding-to-reduce-the-risk-of-harm-and-death-from-substance-use/), [Digit](https://www.digit.fyi/digital-lifelines-bridging-the-gap-for-at-risk-scots/))
- **Never Use Alone** (We Are With You): call while using; if you stop answering, they send help. Started in Glasgow and Ayrshire in 2021. We couldn't confirm it's still running. ([Drink and Drugs News](https://www.drinkanddrugsnews.com/we-are-with-you-launches-never-use-alone-phone-line/), [TFN](https://tfn.scot/news/free-phone-line-launched-to-help-drugs-users-stay-safe))
- **Helplines:** Know the Score, 9am to 9pm on weekdays and shorter at weekends ([Find a Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/organizations/know-the-score)); Scottish Families Affected by Alcohol and Drugs, until 11pm on weekdays with callbacks at weekends ([gov.scot](https://gov.scot/publications/resources-mental-health-wellbeing-primary-care-services-2/pages/10)); Breathing Space, for low mood, 6pm to 2am Monday to Thursday and overnight at weekends ([NHS inform](https://nhsinform.scot/scotlands-service-directory/health-and-wellbeing-services/6e48ea4143f84b21b5cf85fdb2373de1)).
- **The Thistle, Glasgow:** the UK's first safer drug consumption facility, 9am to 9pm, Glasgow only. ([NHS GGC](https://www.nhsggc.scot/glasgow-opens-uks-first-safer-drug-consumption-facility/))
- **Evening drop-ins:** a few, like The Hive in Inverness and a Safebase café in Falkirk. Local and rare. ([Stay Alive](https://stayalive.prevent-suicide.org.uk/find-help-now/resources-by-area/scotland/mikeysline/), [Linlithgow Gazette](https://linlithgowgazette.co.uk/news/people/safehaven-falkirk-evening-natter-cafe-will-provide-support-for-those-in-need-3688543))

Opening hours change often: check each service's own site before quoting them.

## Where the gap is

1. **After 11pm, almost everything is a phone line.** No one puts a trusted person, or a warm place to go, near you in a small town in the early hours.
2. **They all need a working phone.** Digital Lifelines helps, but at 2am on 8% battery there's no fallback like a help point.
3. **Never Use Alone is for the moment of using, not the loneliness before it.** Night Neighbours comes earlier: "I'm alone and low".
4. **Families mostly get their own helpline.** They're rarely part of the person's support in the moment, as Karen is in our prototype.
