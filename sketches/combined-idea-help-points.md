# Combined idea: community help points at night

*Sruthi James and Daniel Lambert-Maddocks, Ctrl-Alt-Elite, 22 October. This combines `idea-locker-help-point.md` (Sruthi) and `daniel-journey-trigger-and-hub.md` (Daniel). It's a sketch for the squad to agree: Bose records the squad's idea in `TEAM.md`.*

## The idea, for Bose to record

> **For people affected by substance use, and the family and friends around them, when they feel alone at night and every service is closed: discreet help points at places they already walk to, like parcel lockers. They can charge their phone, press a button or text a short code to reach a trusted person or an out-of-hours support worker, and stay where they are or go to a nearby hub for the night. Over time, the anonymised totals show councils where more help is needed.**

## What we took from each idea

| From Sruthi's idea | From Daniel's idea |
|---|---|
| Charging points, so a dead phone isn't a dead end | A trigger that works without a phone, for example a code at the locker screen |
| A short text code that needs no data and gives the location by locker, not GPS | Two options: wait where you are for a care worker, or go to a hub |
| Jamie chooses who's told: family (Karen), his support worker (Priya), or both | Being cared for through the night, not just signposted |
| | Anonymised data that shows councils which areas need more funding and support |

## The journey (Jamie, 23:15)

1. **He walks to a help point** at the parcel lockers in his own town. It's lit and close by, so he doesn't need two buses.
2. **He plugs in his phone** to charge.
3. **He asks for help in the way that suits him:**
   - **Texts a short code** (for example `TALK 214`) to Karen or Priya. No data needed.
   - **Presses the help button** to talk to an out-of-hours support worker.
   - **No phone?** Types a code into the locker screen.
4. **The code tells whoever he's chosen where he is** (from the locker number) and what kind of help he wants.
5. **Someone responds, and Jamie chooses:**
   - **Stay:** a support worker or Karen comes to him.
   - **Go:** he's guided to a nearby hub where he can be cared for the night.
6. **The next morning,** with Jamie's agreement, Priya hears what happened, so she's not finding out on Monday.
7. **Over months,** anonymised counts (such as "requests per area per night") show councils and Alcohol and Drug Partnerships where cover is missing. Nothing is ever about one person.

## Example codes (to test)

| Code | Means |
|---|---|
| `TALK` | I want to talk to someone |
| `COME` | Please come and check on me |
| `BED` | I need somewhere to stay tonight |
| `OK` | I'm alright now, thanks |

## Keep in mind

- **Supportive, not an emergency.** It shouldn't feel like 999, and it doesn't replace it. If there's danger to life, it says plainly to call 999.
- **Discreet.** Pressing a button or typing a code in a public place shouldn't single anyone out. Plain wording, no "addiction" signs.
- **Jamie is in control.** He chooses who's told and what's shared. Substance use is special category data.
- **Someone has to answer.** Out-of-hours cover pooled across local charities is the big assumption.
- **No real brands.** In the prototype, call it a "community help point", not Amazon or any real retailer.
