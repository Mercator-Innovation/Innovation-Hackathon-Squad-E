## The merged idea

> **For men aged 35 to 54 in deprived areas who feel isolated, especially in the evenings and at night: an app that connects them to a local hub where they can meet people, be heard and work through problems, with their family and support workers able to support them there. When their phone has no data or battery, help points at places they already walk to, like parcel lockers, let them charge up and reach the hub, their family or a support worker with a short text code. Over time, anonymised totals show councils where more evening help is needed.**

## Three personas and their journeys (added by Daniel)

*Daniel Lambert-Maddocks, added after the first version. It builds on Night Neighbours.*

### The three key personas

1. **The user in need**: someone in a vulnerable situation who needs help, with or without a phone
2. **The family circle**: the people close to them who want to help
3. **The support cohort**: Night Neighbours volunteers and care workers

### The user in need, with a phone

1. They're in a vulnerable situation and need help.
2. They open the Night Neighbours website or app on their phone, or use SMS.
3. The website explains itself, with three choices: **I need someone**, **Go to a help point** and **I'm a volunteer**.

### The user in need, without a phone

- **Offline and digital billboards.** Night Neighbours has key advertising spaces in the real estate of mainstream supermarkets.
- **A locker system** in partnership with mainstream supermarkets, for charging a phone and getting information.
- **A charged phone to pick up.** People without a phone can collect a charged phone donated to charity.
- **A trigger button** at the help point alerts local Night Neighbours care workers.
- **Patrols.** Local Night Neighbours patrol the most affected areas.

### Volunteers (the support cohort)

1. A sign-up journey
2. Access to guidance and training
3. Identifying their local hub

## Keep in mind

- **Supportive, not an emergency.** The hub, app and help points shouldn't feel like 999, and they don't replace it.
- **Discreet.** A help point in a public place mustn't single anyone out.
- **He's in control** of what's shared and with whom. Substance use is special category data.
- **No real brands.** Call them "community help points", not Amazon or any real retailer.
- **Keep it buildable today.** The first version could be the app with the hub, plus one help point screen.
