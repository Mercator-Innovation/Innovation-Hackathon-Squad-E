# Journey idea: a discreet trigger, a night of care, and data that shows where help is needed

*Daniel Lambert-Maddocks, Ctrl-Alt-Elite, 22 October. A sketch for the squad to talk through: not the agreed idea yet.*

**Aim:** leverage data to identify vulnerability in Scotland.

## The journey

1. Someone needs help.
2. They know a trigger to be identified for help (integration to mainstream supermarkets, such as Amazon locker systems).
3. They either stay where they are and wait for a care worker, or they go to a designated hub location.
4. They are able to be cared for the night.
5. Once more data has been gathered over time, we can identify the most problematic areas of Scotland.
6. This can then be communicated to the relevant local authorities for plans for more funding, resource and support.

## Questions to talk through

*Added by Claude from the challenge sheet, for the squad to discuss.*

- **No device needed?** The trigger could work without a personal phone, for example a code at a locker screen or a word said at a till. That fits the "no personal device" personas.
- **Discreet and non-stigmatising:** how does the trigger stay private in a busy supermarket?
- **Supportive, not an emergency:** the sheet says it shouldn't feel like a crisis service or replace 999.
- **Privacy:** substance use is special category data. Steps 5 and 6 could use only anonymised, combined counts (such as "requests per area per night"), never anything about one person.
- **Who answers the trigger at 2am,** and where are the hubs? This links to the gap map idea for the coordinator persona.
- **Would retailers take part?** The squad hasn't checked this. It would be good to find out, and we shouldn't use a real company's brand in the prototype.
