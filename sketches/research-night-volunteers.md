# Research: how vetted community volunteers work at night

*Sruthi James, Ctrl-Alt-Elite, 22 October. Desk research for the squad's idea: an SOS by app or text that reaches vetted volunteers nearby. Not checked with real people yet.*

## What we found

- **Vetting in Scotland is the PVG scheme.** Volunteers with regular or unsupervised contact with "protected adults", meaning people who get care, health or welfare services, need Protecting Vulnerable Groups (PVG) membership. It's free for volunteers, the organisation usually starts the application, and records are updated continuously. Sources: [SCVO](https://scvo.scot/running-your-organisation/legislation/pvg-scheme), [Disclosure Scotland](https://www.disclosure.gov.scot/news/2026/06/free-pvg-scheme-membership-opens-doors-for-scotlands-volunteers). Sources disagree on when the Disclosure (Scotland) Act 2020 requirement started, so check before relying on a date.
- **Night volunteers already exist in Scotland.** Street Pastors in Edinburgh and Aberdeen patrol roughly 22:00 to 04:30 on weekend nights. They listen, give first aid and get people home safely. Training is about 35 to 50 hours, with supervised patrols before going solo. Sources: [Edinburgh Street Pastors](https://streetpastors.org/locations/edinburgh/2023/06/08/commissioning-service-2023/), [Aberdeen Street Pastors](https://streetpastors.org/locations/aberdeen/street-pastors/). They're church-based, which won't suit everyone.
- **An app that alerts the nearest volunteers already works in the UK.** GoodSAM alerts trained volunteers within about 500 to 800 metres of a suspected cardiac arrest. The volunteer accepts or declines, and a decline passes the alert to the next nearest person. Sources: [Welsh Ambulance via GVS](https://www.gvs.wales/news/how-goodsam-is-helping-saves-lives-in-wales), [East of England Ambulance FAQ](https://content.eastamb.nhs.uk/assets/EEAST_Good_Sam_FAQ_d9aae6f52f.pdf). It's triggered by a 999 call, so it's an emergency service, not supportive like ours.
- **A peer-run, 24/7 line for people who use drugs exists in Canada.** The National Overdose Response Service (NORS) is run by people with lived or living experience. The caller makes a plan with a peer operator, which can include a trusted friend or family member being sent to them. If the caller stops responding, the operator calls emergency services. Sources: [CATIE](https://catie.ca/programming-connection/national-overdose-response-service), [Harm Reduction Journal, 2024](https://harmreductionjournal.biomedcentral.com/counter/pdf/10.1186/s12954-024-01017-7.pdf). The family-member part is very close to our Karen persona.

## What it means for our idea

*Our own inference, not from the sources.*

- **Volunteers need PVG checks and training**, so the prototype can show "PVG checked" and "naloxone trained" on volunteer profiles.
- **Copy GoodSAM's accept-or-pass mechanism, but not its emergency feel.** Alert the nearest two or three volunteers, and pass the alert on if they decline.
- **Copy NORS's plan made in advance.** Jamie chooses, before any crisis, who gets told (Karen, Priya, a volunteer), and when to call 999.
- **Peers matter.** NORS is run by people with lived experience. Our volunteers could include peer volunteers in recovery.
