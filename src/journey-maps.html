At the centre: someone on their own late at night, like Jamie, who may have no phone, no data or no battery.

Button: Me

If you'd like some company tonight

Ask the way that suits you: an app, a text, or a help point with no phone needed.

The problem

It's late, you feel low and you're on your own. Most help is closed, and your phone may be dead, out of data, or not there at all.

What it solves

Someone answers at night, however you ask, without forms, without judgement, and you decide who knows.

  1. Before, on a good day

    Make my plan

    Choose who's told, your check-in number and when to check in.

    • Make my plan
  2. On the night

    Ask the way that suits you

    By app, by text on any phone, or at a help point with no phone needed.

    • App
    • Text
    • Help point
  3. Minutes later

    Someone answers

    A checked volunteer nearby offers someone to talk to, someone to come, or somewhere to stay.

  4. Always

    You stay in control

    We only tell the people you pick. See what we keep, for how long, and how to delete it.

    • What we keep
Button: Someone I care about

If you're worried about someone

For family, friends and anyone who wants to point someone our way.

The problem

Families and friends often don't know how to help or where to turn, and professionals can't be there at night.

What it solves

The circle of support becomes part of the help, always with the person's agreement.

  1. If you're family or a friend

    They choose you

    The person adds you to their plan. Nothing is shared without their say-so.

    • Their plan
  2. On the night

    You hear when it matters

    See what you get if they ask for help or miss a check-in, and what you can do next.

    • What family see
  3. If you work in health, care or social work

    Refer someone

    Point someone our way, with their agreement.

    • Refer someone
Button: I volunteer, or would like to

If you'd like to help

Local people who are checked, trained and never sent out alone.

The problem

Services can't cover every night on their own, and people who want to help don't know how, or how to stay safe.

What it solves

A trusted, trained group of local people covers the hours services can't, safely and in pairs.

  1. Step 1

    Sign up

    Become a volunteer, and we start your free PVG (Protecting Vulnerable Groups) check for you.

    • Become a volunteer
  2. Step 2

    Get trained

    Listening, keeping yourself safe, first aid, and naloxone. (An example course: the real one is still to design.)

  3. Step 3

    Go out with someone

    Shadow an experienced volunteer twice. At night you always work in pairs, linked to your local hub.

  4. On shift

    Help nearby

    See requests near you tonight. Say "I can help" or "Not right now". You only see a first name and a way to text if you say yes.

    • Tonight's requests
Button: I work in support or services

If you support people or plan services

Support workers and people who plan local help. You only see what you need, never names.

The problem

Support stops at 5pm, rural areas have the least cover, and planners can't see where help is missing at night.

What it solves

A clean handover into the night and back, cover for places with no one nearby, and totals that show where help should go.

  1. 5pm

    Hand over

    A support worker hands over at 5pm.

    • Support worker
  2. Overnight, anywhere

    No one nearby? Still covered

    A shared night worker rings back within 15 minutes, a peer volunteer rings from another town, help buttons in shops and petrol stations, and a van on set nights.

    • When there's no one nearby
  3. Next morning

    Hear about the night

    The support worker hears what happened, with the person's agreement.

    • Support worker
  4. Over time

    See where help is needed

    Totals by area and time, never names. Areas short of volunteers are flagged for a person to check.

    • I plan local support
Button: I help spread the word

Spreading the word

How people find out about Night Neighbours before the night they need it.

The problem

An app nobody's heard of doesn't help, and many people who need this don't go online.

What it solves

People already know what to do, and where to go, before the night they need it.

  1. In everyday places

    See it around town

    Posters, beer mats and posts that tell people we're here.

    • Posters and posts
  2. For people offline

    Keep a card

    A wallet card and a poster, for people who don't go online.

    • Wallet card and poster
  3. On the night

    Know what to do

    Text a code, find a help point, or open the app.

    • Text
    • Help point
    • App
